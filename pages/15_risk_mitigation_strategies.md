# Ways to handle risk

::left::

## Avoid

- Stop doing the risky activity
- Remove the vulnerability entirely

## Reduce

- Add controls to lower likelihood or impact
- Most common approach

::right::

## Transfer

- Shift the risk to someone else
- E.g. insurance, outsourcing

## Accept

- Do nothing further
- Used when risk is low or cost to fix is too high

# Common mitigation strategies

::left::

## Technical controls

- Regular backups
- Software and OS updates
- Multi-factor authentication
- Firewalls and antivirus
- Encryption (both at rest and in transit)

::right::

## People and process controls

- Staff training and awareness
- Clear security policies
- Limiting access to what's needed
- Incident response plan
- Disaster recovery plan

# Principle of Least Privilege

The **Principle of Least Privilege** is a security concept that states that users should only have the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job functions. This helps reduce the risk of accidental or intentional misuse of sensitive information and systems.

The closer a user's access level is to the minimum required, the lower the risk of a security breach.

# Incident Response Plan

An **Incident Response Plan (IRP)** explains what to do when a security incident occurs.

Its goal is to limit the damage from incidents such as data breaches, malware, or denial-of-service attacks.

An IRP usually includes:

- Preparation: Set up policies, procedures, and resources.
- Identification: Detect and confirm the incident.
- Containment: Limit the damage and prevent it from spreading.
- Review and Respond: Find the cause and prevent it from happening again. Communicate with affected parties and stakeholders.

# Disaster Recovery Plan

A **Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)** explains how to recover from a disaster or major disruption.

It usually includes:

- Backups and recovery steps for critical data and systems.
- Alternative communication methods and key contacts.
- Steps for restoring IT systems and services.
