# Feasibility

> **Feasibility** (noun): the extent to which an idea is possible, practical, and achievable.

## Feasibility Analysis

> **Feasibility analysis** is the process of evaluating a proposed idea or project to determine whether it is viable and worth pursuing. It involves assessing various factors such as technical, financial, operational, and legal feasibility to make informed decisions about the project's potential success.

# Technical Feasibility

**Technical feasibility** assesses whether the proposed idea can be implemented using existing technology and resources. It considers factors such as the availability of necessary tools, expertise, and infrastructure.

- **Not At All:** The technology does not exist and is unlikely to be developed soon.
- **Low:** The technology is still being developed or is not widely available, making the idea hard to implement.
- **Medium:** The idea can use existing technology but may need some changes or extra resources.
- **High:** The idea can use available technology and resources with little or no changes.

# Financial Feasibility

**Financial feasibility** assesses whether the proposed idea is affordable and likely to provide value. It considers costs, funding, expected revenue, and financial risks.

- **Not At All:** The costs are too high, and there is no clear way to fund the idea.
- **Low:** The idea may need more funding than is available or may provide little financial value.
- **Medium:** The idea is affordable with careful planning or additional funding.
- **High:** The idea is affordable and is likely to provide strong financial value.

# Operational Feasibility

**Operational feasibility** assesses whether the organization can support and use the proposed idea. It considers processes, people, training, and day-to-day requirements.

- **Not At All:** The organization cannot support or operate the idea.
- **Low:** The idea would require major changes to people, processes, or resources.
- **Medium:** The organization can support the idea with some changes and training.
- **High:** The idea fits current operations and can be adopted with little disruption.

---
layout: center
---

# Legal Feasibility

**Legal feasibility** assesses whether the proposed idea follows relevant laws, regulations, contracts, and policies. It also considers legal risks and required approvals.

- **Not At All:** The idea conflicts with laws or regulations and cannot be approved.
- **Low:** The idea has serious legal risks or requires major changes to meet requirements.
- **Medium:** The idea can meet legal requirements with some review or changes.
- **High:** The idea meets legal requirements and has low legal risk.

# Performing A Feasibility Analysis

- Copy the feasibility matrix and identify the level of feasibility for each factor (technical, financial, operational, legal) based on your research and analysis.
- Provide a summary sentence - identifying the overall feasibility of your idea and whether it is worth pursuing further.
  - Include possible modifications to the idea that might improve feasibility.
- Repeat for each of your ideas and compare results.
