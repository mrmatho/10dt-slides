# Vocabulary: Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risks

::left::

<div class="note">

A **threat** is a potential danger that could cause harm to a system or organisation.

A **vulnerability** is a weakness in a system that can be exploited by a threat.

A **risk** is the assessment of how likely a threat is to exploit a vulnerability and the potential impact it could have.

</div>

::right::



### Example

Boris opens an email from an unknown sender and clicks a link.

<v-clicks>

- Boris' lack of awareness that the email could be malicious is a **vulnerability**.
- The fact that the email could be a phishing attempt or contain malware is a **threat**.
- The potential for Boris' computer to be infected and sensitive data to be stolen is the **risk**

</v-clicks>

# Check your understanding - Answer

**Threat:** Malicious code gets injected into the system.

**Vulnerability:** The web application does not properly check user input, which is a weakness that can be exploited.

**Risk:** If an attacker successfully injects malicious code, it could lead to data breaches, system compromise, or other negative consequences for the company and its users.

# Threat types

We classify cybersecurity threats into three main categories:


## Accidental threats

Accidental threats occur when harm is caused **unintentionally**, often due to human error or system malfunctions. Examples include: 

- A user accidentally deleting important files
- A software bug that causes a system crash

::right::

## Deliberate threats

Deliberate threats are **intentional** actions taken by individuals or groups to cause harm to a system or organization. Examples include:

- Hackers attempting to gain unauthorized access to a system
- Employees stealing sensitive data for personal gain

## Event-based threats

Event-based threats are **unforeseen events** that can cause harm to a system or organization. Examples include:

- Natural disasters such as earthquakes or floods
- Power outages that disrupt system operations

# Controls

We can implement **controls** to mitigate the risks associated with cybersecurity threats. Controls can be classified by whether these controls are **physical** or **sofware** security controls. 

::left::

**Physical controls** protect the physical assets of an organization, such as buildings, equipment, and personnel. Examples include:

- Security guards and surveillance cameras
- Access control systems, such as key cards or biometric scanners

::right::

**Software controls** protect the digital assets of an organization, such as data, applications, and networks. Examples include:

- Firewalls and intrusion detection systems
- Encryption and secure coding practices
