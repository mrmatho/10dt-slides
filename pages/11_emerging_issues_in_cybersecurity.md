# Stakeholders in Cybersecurity

A **stakeholder** is a person, group, or organization that has an interest in a particular system or technology.

Anyone who can be affected by its security vulnerabilities, threats, and risks is a stakeholder.

Clearly identifying stakeholders helps to more effectively describe the threats and risks to a system, and to be able to develop strategies to mitigate those risks.

# Review: Vulnerabilities, Threats, and Risks

- **Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses in a system that can be exploited by attackers to gain unauthorized access or cause harm.
- **Threats** are potential dangers that can exploit vulnerabilities to cause harm or damage to a system.
  - **Deliberate threats** are intentional attacks, such as hacking or malware
  - **Accidental threats** are unintentional events, such as human error or unforeseen technical failures
  - **Event-based threats** are threats that arise from specific events, such as natural disasters or power outages
- **Risks** are the potential consequences of a threat exploiting a vulnerability, which can result in **harm**, and the likelihood of that harm occurring.

# Identifying the Important Information

When identifying vulnerabilities, threats, and risks, you can ask yourself the following questions:

::left::

### Vulnerabilities

- What has gone wrong? (or what could go wrong?)
- Is there already any evidence of a security hole or vulnerability?
- Can I see any problems with the security planning or processes that might cause or increase a vulnerability?

::right::

### Threats

- Who is likely to make use of this vulnerability? (deliberate)
- What could go wrong by accident? (accidental)
- Are there obvious events that might apply specifically to this system/vulnerability? (event-based)



### Risks

- What is the worst that can happen?
- What is the likelihood of that happening?
- What makes it more or less likely to happen?
